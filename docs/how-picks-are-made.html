How The Five Picks Are Made

Two filters and one score. A name is removed by any gate it fails, and whatever survives is ranked on four measures — then the top five are sized and emailed.

The funnel

Three stages on three different clocks. The expensive work happens once a week; the daily runs only re-check forty names.

How one name becomes a pick

Gates are pass/fail and run first — no amount of score rescues a failed gate. Only survivors are scored, and scoring is relative: z-scores are computed across whatever set survived that day.

What happens to a pick you buy

Sizing. stop% = 3.0 × the stock's 20-day daily sigma, then size £ = min(5% of account ÷ stop%, 25% of account, cash). On typical small-cap volatility the 25% cap binds, so four positions is a fully-invested book. The £ figure converts to dollars at the live rate plus your broker's FX fee.

Exits, in order. Stop hit, or 21 trading days elapsed. Nothing else — no profit target, no trailing stop.

Alerts. A close below the 20-day average, a breached stop, earnings moving inside five days, or day 19 of 21 each send their own email. The 20-day alert is a prompt to look, not an instruction to sell: tested as an exit rule it cut the median month by £2 and quadrupled the number of trades.

Monitoring only sees what is logged. A position bought and not recorded on the desk gets no stop check, no alert and no hold-period countdown.
